\begin{abstract}
\noindent
NeuralDMD now recovers the $m\!=\!2$ EVPA swirl of a polarized thick ring:
$\mathrm{EVPA}=1.8^\circ$, $\beta_2$ amplitude $0.94$ at phase $-0.0^\circ$, with
$\chi^2$ at the data's own noise floor---using the \emph{plain fractional}
parameterization and \emph{no polarization prior at all}. Adding a Stokes-$I$
compactness prior additionally recovers the orbiting hot spot
($\mathrm{NRMSE}_I=0.17$, $75\%$ of the true on-ring variability).
This note supersedes an earlier version, which concluded that fractional
polarization was structurally incapable of representing $m\!=\!2$ and adopted an
$I$-scaled chart instead. That conclusion was wrong. It rested on an ``off-source
polarized haze'' that was not a property of the model or the data but an artifact
of the \emph{image export path}. Section~\ref{sec:bug} documents the bug, because
everything else in the effort was downstream of it.
\end{abstract}

\section{The bug: a phantom haze in the exported images}
\label{sec:bug}

Training evaluates the sky model inside a JIT-compiled region
(\texttt{eqx.filter\_jit}). The evaluation path---\texttt{reconstruct\_polarized\_cubes},
which produces the cubes that every metric and every figure reads---called the same
function \emph{eagerly}. The two do not agree. Measured on one fixed model, same
inputs, same GPU:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
\texttt{stokes\_fields} evaluation & flux/frame [Jy] & $\chi^2_{RR}$ \\
\midrule
eager (the export path)          & 9.9536 & 137.743 \\
jitted (what training optimizes) & 2.6255 & \phantom{0}9.565 \\
\midrule
truth                            & 2.7    & \phantom{00}0.4 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The eager path adds a near-uniform positive offset of ${\sim}3\times10^{-3}$ per
pixel---about $7.3$~Jy of spurious diffuse flux per frame on a $50\times50$ grid.
It is invisible per pixel and enormous in the integral. It is not a precision
effect: forcing \texttt{Precision.HIGHEST} and
\texttt{default\_matmul\_precision("highest")} changes nothing, and the complex
einsum and the $\chi^2$ reduction are bit-identical eager vs.\ jitted.

\paragraph{Consequence.} Every exported cube, and therefore every NRMSE, EVPA and
$\beta_2$ ever recorded in this effort, described an image carrying $7.3$~Jy of
phantom haze, while the model being trained was clean. \textbf{That offset is the
``off-source polarized haze.''} The support prior, the radial polarized compactness
prior, the $I$-scaled and matrix-exponential charts, and an $L_1$ prior on polarized
flux were all designed to suppress it. They were fighting a rendering artifact. So
was the ``winding obstruction'' argument: fractional polarization recovers the swirl
to $1.8^\circ$.

\paragraph{Fix.} Evaluate under JIT in the export path, exactly as training does.
Two guards now make this class of error impossible to miss: an in-run self-check
that recomputes $\chi^2$ directly from the exported cube and compares it against
both the reported $\chi^2$ and the truth's floor; and a regression test asserting
\emph{cube $=$ jitted field} by \textbf{total flux}. The flux integral is the
essential part---a uniform offset is invisible to a max-pixel comparison, which is
why the bug survived several rounds of ``the images are identical'' checks.

\section{The method}

\paragraph{Sky model.} The standard NeuralDMD decomposition per Stokes field,
\begin{equation}
  I(x,t) \;=\; W_0(x)\,b_0 \;+\; 2\,\mathrm{Re}\!\sum_{j=1}^{r} W_j(x)\,b_j\,
  e^{\Omega_j t_{\mathrm{scale}} t},
\end{equation}
with a coordinate MLP producing the static mode $W_0$ and the $r$ complex dynamic
spatial modes $W_j$, and small MLPs producing the spectrum $\Omega_j$ and
amplitudes $b_j$.

\paragraph{Polarization: fractional, unmodified.}
\begin{equation}
  Q = m_\ell\,I\cos 2\xi, \qquad U = m_\ell\,I\sin 2\xi,
\end{equation}
with $m_\ell=\texttt{scaling\_ml}\cdot\sigma(\text{raw}-\texttt{outshift})$ and the
EVPA direction $(\cos2\xi,\sin2\xi)$ from its own networks. This is the original
chart. It gives $P\le I$ and $Q,U\to0$ as $I\to0$ for free, and---contrary to the
previous version of this note---it reaches $m\!=\!2$ without difficulty.

\paragraph{Likelihood.} Reduced $\chi^2$ on the native circular products, with
$RR=I+V$, $LL=I-V$, $RL=Q+iU$, $LR=Q-iU$, each
$\chi^2=\sum|\Delta|^2\,\mathrm{mask}/\sigma^2 \,/\, (2\sum\mathrm{mask})$ (per real
degree of freedom; identical to \texttt{ehtim}'s \texttt{chisq\_vis}). We never
divide by $I$.

\paragraph{Priors.} \emph{None on polarization.} On Stokes $I$: a total-flux
(lightcurve) anchor and a compactness prior
$\big\langle\sum_x \mathrm{relu}(I)\,r^2\big\rangle$, both needed only for the
\emph{dynamic} part (Section~\ref{sec:dyn}).

\paragraph{Configuration.} The two runs below differ only in the $I$ priors and the
spatial encoding:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
 & pol-only (\texttt{FIXa}) & pol + dynamics (\texttt{cmp10}) \\
\midrule
\texttt{--pol-param}        & \texttt{fractional} & \texttt{fractional} \\
\texttt{--r} / \texttt{--r-pol} & 8 / full (unstarved) & 8 / full \\
\texttt{--i-only-epochs}    & 0 (joint, not staged) & 0 \\
\texttt{--frequencies}      & 2 & \textbf{4} \\
\texttt{--flux} / \texttt{--flux-weight} & off & \textbf{2.7 / 3} \\
\texttt{--compact-weight}   & 0 & \textbf{10} \\
\texttt{--early-stop-chi2}  & 0 (disabled) & 0 (disabled) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Common to both: $12000$ epochs, AdamW, lr $3\times10^{-4}$ decaying $0.5$ per
$25000$ steps, hidden $256\times4$, \texttt{outshift} $2.0$,
\texttt{scaling\_ml} $1.0$, \texttt{theta\_max} $1.0$, npix $50$,
FOV $200\,\mu$as, $64$ frames, batch $8$, seed $0$, disk pretrain $2000$ steps.

\paragraph{Two settings that matter and are not obvious.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Disable early stopping.} The default threshold ($\chi^2\le0.8$)
  sits \emph{above} the truth's own $\chi^2$ (Section~\ref{sec:floor}), so training
  halts before convergence.
  \item \textbf{Do not starve polarization.} Reducing the polarized mode count
  (\texttt{--r-pol 2}) or fitting $I$ first and freezing it were both introduced as
  anti-overfit measures; neither is needed, and the swirl is recovered without them.
\end{itemize}

\section{The $\chi^2$ floor is $0.4$, not $1$}
\label{sec:floor}

Dataset generation calls \texttt{obs.add\_fractional\_noise(0.04)}, whose ehtim
implementation \emph{inflates $\sigma$ without adding matching noise to the
visibilities}: $\sigma \leftarrow \sqrt{\sigma^2 + (0.04\,|V|)^2}$. The residual
therefore stays thermal while the error bars grow, and \textbf{the truth itself
scores $\chi^2\approx0.42$}. Pushing the truth through the forward operator
reproduces this on every product ($0.41/0.42/0.37/0.41$), which is also how we
validate the operator. Two consequences: a $\chi^2$ gate centred on $1$ can never
pass (it is now $[0.4,2]$), and any early-stop threshold must be set relative to the
achievable floor. Every run reports \texttt{chi2\_truth\_floor} alongside its own
$\chi^2$.

\section{Results}

Synthetic polarized thick m-ring (\texttt{mring+hsCW}): $20\%$ spiral-EVPA linear
polarization, an unpolarized Gaussian hot spot orbiting at $r\approx28\,\mu$as,
observed with the EHT 2017 array. $\beta_2$ is the Palumbo $m\!=\!2$ azimuthal
coefficient of $Q+iU$ over the ring annulus (the EHT/KINE global EVPA measure);
truth $|\beta_2| = 0.180$. ``DYN'' is the summed per-pixel temporal standard
deviation of $I$, split inside the ring annulus and beyond $50\,\mu$as.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccccc@{}}
\toprule
 & $\chi^2_{RR}$ & NRMSE$_I$ & EVPA & $\beta_2$ amp & DYN on-ring & DYN off-src \\
\midrule
truth                    & 0.41 & 0     & $0^\circ$   & 1.00  & 0.355 & 0.003 \\
\midrule
\texttt{FIXa} (no priors)& 0.78 & 0.286 & $\mathbf{1.8^\circ}$ & $\mathbf{0.941}$ & 0.152 & 0.682 \\
\texttt{cmp10}           & 0.49 & $\mathbf{0.169}$ & $3.7^\circ$ & 0.902 & 0.267 & 0.161 \\
\texttt{cmp30}           & 0.71 & 0.189 & $5.4^\circ$ & 0.879 & $\mathbf{0.308}$ & $\mathbf{0.125}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Polarization is recovered by the plain chart with no polarized prior, and is robust:
across every configuration tried (spatial frequency $2$--$4$, mode count $8$--$16$,
compactness $0$--$30$) EVPA stays within $1.8$--$5.4^\circ$ and $\beta_2$ within
$0.88$--$1.36$.

\section{Recovering the hot spot}
\label{sec:dyn}

With polarization solved, the remaining error is the \emph{dynamic} part of
Stokes~$I$. The truth's variability lies entirely on the ring ($0.355$ on-ring,
$0.003$ beyond $50\,\mu$as). Without $I$ priors the reconstruction produces
\emph{more} total variability than the truth but spreads it over ${\sim}77\,\mu$as
instead of concentrating it in an orbiting spot: only $43\%$ on-ring, and $0.682$
off-source---over two orders of magnitude too much. Two things fix it.

\paragraph{Spatial bandwidth.} \texttt{--frequencies 2} makes the finest
representable feature ${\sim}\mathrm{FOV}/2^2 = 50\,\mu$as, but the hot spot is a
compact blob orbiting at $r\approx28\,\mu$as: the network cannot resolve it and
approximates it with broad low-frequency wobble. \texttt{--frequencies 4} lowers the
floor to ${\sim}12.5\,\mu$as.

\paragraph{Compactness, at the right weight.} Higher bandwidth \emph{alone} makes
matters worse---it lets more variability leak off-source. It must be paired with the
compactness prior, whose weight was $10$--$30\times$ too low. The two work together
and improve $I$, its dynamics, and the haze monotonically:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\texttt{--compact-weight} (at \texttt{--frequencies 4}) & NRMSE$_I$ & DYN on-ring & DYN off-src \\
\midrule
0 (and \texttt{--frequencies 2}) & 0.286 & 0.152 & 0.682 \\
3   & 0.219 & 0.210 & 0.379 \\
10  & \textbf{0.169} & 0.267 & 0.161 \\
30  & 0.189 & \textbf{0.308} & \textbf{0.125} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
There is a mild cost to polarization as the weight rises (EVPA $1.8^\circ\to5.4^\circ$,
$\beta_2$ $0.94\to0.88$), so the setting is a balance rather than a maximum;
\texttt{--compact-weight 10} is the current recommendation.

\section{What is still open}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{NRMSE$_{Q,U}\approx0.26$--$0.30$} against a $0.15$ target. The target
  may be unrealistic for this coverage: KINE reports the global $\angle\beta_2$ and
  explicitly not per-pixel EVPA.
  \item \textbf{Off-source dynamic haze} $0.125$--$0.161$ vs.\ a truth of $0.003$.
  Unlike the static haze of Section~\ref{sec:bug} this one is real: it survives the
  fix and lives in the short-baseline null space, where the array has no leverage.
  \item \textbf{Gains and D-terms} are not in this forward model. Gain-corrupted data
  cannot be fit by a gain-free model---a first attempt on a gain-corrupted on-sky
  UVFITS reached $\chi^2_{RR}\approx1380$. \texttt{StationGains} exists on a separate
  branch and is not yet wired into the polarized likelihood.
  \item Several parameterizations (\texttt{direct}, \texttt{iscaled}, \texttt{expm},
  \texttt{expm\_full}) and priors (support, radial polarized compactness, polarized
  $L_1$, dynamic-mode compactness) remain in the codebase, off by default. They were
  built against the phantom haze and, on present evidence, are unnecessary. They are
  retained rather than removed pending re-evaluation on the corrected export.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Methodological note}

The haze was accepted as physics for four working sessions and motivated an entire
family of parameterizations and priors. It was never real. What made it durable is
that the reported $\chi^2$ was correct while the images were wrong---nothing in the
pipeline compared the object being optimized against the object being measured. The
two guards of Section~\ref{sec:bug} now assert exactly that equality, and the one
that matters compares \emph{integrated flux}: the discrepancy was a uniform offset,
which every per-pixel comparison we ran reported as ``identical''.
