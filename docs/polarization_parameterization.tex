\begin{abstract}
\noindent
NeuralDMD recovers polarized black-hole movies from sparse EHT visibilities using the
\emph{plain fractional} chart $(I,m_\ell,\xi)$ and \emph{no polarization prior at all}.
On the four ehteval test models, one configuration gives EVPA
$3.7^\circ/3.6^\circ/1.9^\circ/9.6^\circ$ with $\chi^2$ at the data's own noise floor.
The rotating-EVPA model (\texttt{mring-varbeta2}) is recovered essentially exactly
($\beta_2$ amplitude $1.00$, phase correlation $+1.000$ across a $1620^\circ$ swing).
Two ingredients are non-obvious and both are necessary: a Stokes-$I$ compactness prior
at a weight $10$--$30\times$ larger than we had been using, and a compactness prior on
the \emph{dynamic modes only} at a weight $\sim10^{3}\times$ smaller than a naive
guess. One model, \texttt{mring+hs-pol} (a polarized \emph{orbiting} spot), is not
recovered by any chart. This note supersedes two earlier versions, both of which drew
their conclusions from an image-export bug rather than from the data
(Section~\ref{sec:bug}).
\end{abstract}

\section{The method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Sky model.} The NeuralDMD decomposition, per Stokes field,
\begin{equation}
  I(x,t) \;=\; W_0(x)\,b_0 \;+\; 2\,\mathrm{Re}\!\sum_{j=1}^{r} W_j(x)\,b_j\,
  e^{\Omega_j t_{\mathrm{scale}} t},
  \label{eq:dmd}
\end{equation}
with a coordinate MLP producing the static mode $W_0$ and the $r$ complex dynamic
spatial modes $W_j$, and small MLPs producing the spectrum $\Omega_j$ and amplitudes
$b_j$.

\paragraph{Polarization: fractional, unmodified.}
\begin{equation}
  Q = m_\ell\,I\cos 2\xi, \qquad U = m_\ell\,I\sin 2\xi, \qquad V = m_c\,I,
\end{equation}
with $m_\ell=\texttt{scaling\_ml}\cdot\sigma(\text{raw}-\texttt{outshift})$ and the EVPA
direction $(\cos2\xi,\sin2\xi)$ from its own networks. Polarization is tied to $I$ and
starts unpolarized, growing only as the cross-hand data demand it. This is one of five
charts we implemented and ran; Section~\ref{sec:charts} gives all five and why this one
ships.

\paragraph{Likelihood.} Reduced $\chi^2$ on the native circular products
($RR=I+V$, $LL=I-V$, $RL=Q+iU$, $LR=Q-iU$), each
$\sum|\Delta|^2\,\mathrm{mask}/\sigma^2/(2\sum\mathrm{mask})$ --- per real degree of
freedom, identical to \texttt{ehtim}'s \texttt{chisq\_vis}. We never divide by $I$.

\paragraph{The configuration.} Identical for every model:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
\texttt{--pol-param fractional}      & the original chart; no pol prior of any kind \\
\texttt{--r 8}, \texttt{--r-pol} full & do \emph{not} starve polarized capacity \\
\texttt{--i-only-epochs 0}           & joint, not staged \\
\texttt{--frequencies 4}             & finest feature ${\sim}$FOV$/2^4=12.5\,\mu$as \\
\texttt{--flux 2.7 --flux-weight 3}  & lightcurve anchor (no zero-spacing baseline) \\
\texttt{--compact-weight 10}         & Stokes-$I$ compactness (Section~\ref{sec:dyn}) \\
\texttt{--dyn-compact-weight 1e-3}   & dynamic-mode compactness (Section~\ref{sec:dyn}) \\
\texttt{--early-stop-chi2 0}         & disabled (Section~\ref{sec:floor}) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
12000 epochs, AdamW, lr $3\times10^{-4}$ decaying $0.5$ per $25000$ steps, hidden
$256\times4$, \texttt{outshift} $2.0$, \texttt{theta\_max} $1.0$, npix $50$,
FOV $200\,\mu$as, 64 frames, batch 8, seed 0.

\section{The five polarization charts}
\label{sec:charts}

Every chart consumes the same raw sub-networks --- \texttt{intensity}, \texttt{frac},
\texttt{cos2xi}, \texttt{sin2xi}, and optionally \texttt{circ} for $V$ --- each an
independent NeuralDMD field of the form~\eqref{eq:dmd}. They differ \emph{only} in the
algebraic map from those raws to $(Q,U,V)$. Fields a chart does not need are kept live as
spares, so the freeze/warm-up partition and the sparsity mode list are identical across
charts: changing \texttt{--pol-param} changes the algebra and nothing else. Below,
$\sigma$ is the logistic sigmoid and $\epsilon$ a numerical floor.

Two design axes organize the five, and they turn out to be the two that predict the
results:

\begin{description}
  \item[Split.] Is the polarized \emph{magnitude} carried by its own scalar field --- with
  its own DMD expansion, hence its own modes and its own $\Omega_j$ --- or is it
  $|(q,u)|$, a nonlinear function of two other fields? Under a split, a moving polarized
  blob is a moving blob \emph{in one field}. Without one, the same blob must be encoded as
  correlated motion in two fields that are then combined nonlinearly.
  \item[Winding.] Is the EVPA direction pinned to the unit circle $S^1$? If it is, its
  winding number around the ring is a discrete topological invariant: changing it during
  optimization forces the raw vector through the origin, where the normalized direction is
  undefined and the gradient is singular. That is an \emph{optimization barrier}, not a
  representational limit --- the pattern is expressible, but gradient descent must tunnel
  to reach it.
\end{description}

\paragraph{1. \texttt{fractional} --- split, with winding. (Default; shipped.)}
\begin{equation}
  m_\ell = \texttt{scaling\_ml}\cdot\sigma(m_{\mathrm{raw}}-\texttt{outshift})
  \in [0,\texttt{scaling\_ml}],
  \qquad
  (\cos2\xi,\,\sin2\xi) = \frac{(c_{\mathrm{raw}},\,s_{\mathrm{raw}})}
       {\sqrt{c_{\mathrm{raw}}^{2}+s_{\mathrm{raw}}^{2}+\epsilon}},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  Q = m_\ell\,I\cos2\xi,\qquad U = m_\ell\,I\sin2\xi,\qquad
  V = m_c\,I,\quad m_c = 2\big(\sigma(v_{\mathrm{raw}}-\texttt{outshift})-\tfrac12\big).
\end{equation}
\emph{What it does.} Separates magnitude ($m_\ell$, one field) from direction (a unit
vector). Because the direction is normalized, $P_{\mathrm{lin}}=\sqrt{Q^2+U^2}=m_\ell|I|$
\emph{exactly}, so $P_{\mathrm{lin}}\le\texttt{scaling\_ml}\cdot|I|\le I$ strictly, by
construction and with no penalty. Support is automatic ($Q,U\propto I$). The
$-\texttt{outshift}$ offset starts $m_\ell\approx0$, so the fit begins unpolarized and
grows polarization only as the cross-hands demand it. \emph{Caveat:} the strict bound is
on \emph{linear} polarization only --- $m_\ell$ and $m_c$ are bounded independently, so
once $V$ is enabled $\sqrt{Q^2+U^2+V^2}=|I|\sqrt{m_\ell^2+m_c^2}$ can exceed $I$. \emph{The
cost:} the unit normalization is exactly the winding barrier.

\paragraph{2. \texttt{direct} --- no split, no constraints.}
\begin{equation}
  Q,\,U \;=\; \text{independent signed NeuralDMD fields, scaled to Stokes-$I$ units}.
\end{equation}
\emph{What it does.} Removes every structural constraint: no magnitude/direction
decomposition at all, so no winding barrier and any EVPA pattern is as cheap as any other.
\emph{Why not:} it also removes the two constraints worth having. Nothing enforces $P\le I$
and nothing ties $Q,U$ to $I$'s support, so polarization can form off-source where no
Stokes~$I$ exists. Both are demoted to a soft \texttt{p\_weight} penalty that must fight
the likelihood rather than bound it.

\paragraph{3. \texttt{iscaled} --- no split, support by construction.}
\begin{equation}
  q = \tanh q_{\mathrm{raw}},\quad u = \tanh u_{\mathrm{raw}},\qquad
  Q = I\,q,\qquad U = I\,u,\qquad V = I\tanh v_{\mathrm{raw}}.
\end{equation}
\emph{What it does.} Ties polarization to $I$ multiplicatively, so $Q,U\to0$ wherever
$I\to0$: the support constraint holds \emph{by construction} rather than through a penalty,
and no off-source haze can form. $q,u$ stay free, so there is no winding barrier.
\emph{Why not:} $P=I\sqrt{q^2+u^2}\le\sqrt2\,I$ --- the bound is $\sqrt2$, not $1$, so the
residual $P\le I$ still needs the soft penalty.

\paragraph{4. \texttt{expm} --- no split, exact $P\le I$.}
Arras et al.\ (2025), the form \texttt{resolve} uses, applied on top of our own $I$ field:
\begin{equation}
  p = \sqrt{q^2+u^2+v^2+\epsilon},\qquad
  (Q,U,V) = I\,\frac{\tanh p}{p}\,(q,u,v).
\end{equation}
\emph{What it does.} $m=\tanh p\le1$ gives \emph{exact} $P\le I$ (cleaner than
\texttt{iscaled}'s $\sqrt2$), support $\propto I$, no winding ($q,u,v$ are free and
$\tanh(p)/p$ is smooth through $p=0$), and native $V$. No $P\le I$ penalty required. On
paper this dominates \texttt{iscaled} on every axis.

\paragraph{5. \texttt{expm\_full} --- the full matrix exponential.}
The complete coherency matrix, with $s$ the \emph{log} intensity:
\begin{equation}
  X = \exp\!\begin{pmatrix} s+v & q+iu \\ q-iu & s-v \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  I = e^{s}\cosh p, \qquad (Q,U,V) = e^{s}\,\frac{\sinh p}{p}\,(q,u,v).
\end{equation}
\emph{What it does.} $X=\exp(\text{Hermitian})$ is positive semi-definite \emph{by
construction}: $I>0$ exactly (no negativity penalty needed) and
$\det X = I^2-P^2\ge0$, i.e.\ $P\le I$ \emph{everywhere} --- not merely where a separately
parameterized $I$ happens to stay positive. This is the physically complete matrix a
correct polarized RIME (gains, D-terms, feed rotation) acts on, so it is the natural
endpoint for calibration work. \emph{The catch:} $I$ is now log-parameterized. It is no
longer the field the two Stokes-$I$ priors (Section~\ref{sec:dyn}) were tuned against, and
it is incompatible with the physical-$I$ disk pretrain (\texttt{--no-pretrain}). $p$ and
$s$ are clipped to keep $\cosh/\sinh$ inside float32.

The ehteval suite, each testing a different axis. $\beta_2$ is the Palumbo $m\!=\!2$
azimuthal coefficient of $Q+iU$ over the ring annulus (truth $|\beta_2|=0.180$);
``$\beta_2(t)$ corr'' is the correlation of the \emph{unwrapped per-frame} $\beta_2$

\texttt{mring+hsCCW} matches \texttt{mring+hsCW} within noise: \textbf{no direction

\paragraph{Why \emph{fractional}, when other charts score better on individual models.}
We ran the full grid: 4 models $\times$ 5 parameterizations, one configuration, no
per-cell tuning. Ranked by the \emph{worst} case rather than the best:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
parameterization & EVPA across the four models & worst & gates passed ($\chi^2$ / EVPA) \\
\midrule
\textbf{fractional} & 3.7 / 3.6 / 1.9 / \textbf{9.6} & $\mathbf{9.6^\circ}$ & \textbf{4/4} \; / \; \textbf{4/4} \\
\texttt{expm}       & 1.7 / 1.9 / 1.0 / 15.5 & $15.5^\circ$ & 4/4 \; / \; 3/4 \\
\texttt{iscaled}    & 2.4 / 1.7 / 1.3 / 19.0 & $19.0^\circ$ & 4/4 \; / \; 3/4 \\
\texttt{direct}     & 2.9 / 2.0 / 2.7 / 13.5 & $13.5^\circ$ & 4/4 \; / \; 3/4 \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\texttt{expm} and \texttt{iscaled} are \emph{better} than fractional on the three
easier models (sharper EVPA, and they reach NRMSE$_{Q,U}\le0.15$ on
\texttt{varbeta2}), then fail \texttt{hs-pol} at $15.5^\circ$ and $19.0^\circ$.
\texttt{expm\_full} additionally breaks \texttt{hsCW} outright ($\chi^2=2.23$, and its
dynamics overshoot the truth: on-ring variability $0.545$ against $0.355$).
\textbf{Fractional is the only chart that never breaks}, so it is the one to ship when
a single pipeline must cover everything. If only the three easier models matter,
\texttt{expm} is the better choice --- this is a genuine trade, not a ranking.

\paragraph{The two axes sort the grid.} The ranking is not arbitrary: \emph{split} and
\emph{winding} (Section~\ref{sec:charts}) each predict exactly where they should matter,
and each does.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Winding costs fractional precisely where winding is required.} On
  \texttt{varbeta2} --- whose EVPA turns through $1620^\circ$, the one model that
  \emph{demands} a large winding number --- every barrier-free chart beats fractional
  ($1.0^\circ$, $1.0^\circ$, $1.3^\circ$, $2.7^\circ$ versus $1.9^\circ$), and the two
  exact-$P\!\le\!I$ ones reach $1.0^\circ$. The barrier is real and it is measurable. It
  is also \emph{small}: fractional still recovers this model at $\beta_2=1.002$ and phase
  correlation $+1.000$, so the barrier is tunnelled, not fatal.
  \item \textbf{The split pays precisely where magnitude dynamics are required.} On
  \texttt{hs-pol} --- a compact polarized blob that \emph{moves}, i.e.\ dynamics in the
  polarized \emph{magnitude} --- fractional is best at $9.6^\circ$ and \emph{all four}
  unsplit charts land at $13.5$--$19.0^\circ$. Under a split the moving blob is a moving
  blob in the single field $m_\ell$, which owns its own DMD modes and $\Omega_j$; without
  one, the same blob must appear as correlated motion in $q$ and $u$ that only becomes
  localized after the nonlinear combination $|(q,u)|$.
\end{itemize}
The winding claim rests on four independent charts agreeing, and is solid. The split claim
rests on a single chart carrying \texttt{split}${=}$\texttt{yes}, so it is a
\textbf{hypothesis consistent with all five results}, not an established mechanism ---
$n{=}1$ on the axis that matters most. It is, however, a \emph{falsifiable} one, which
leads to the obvious next experiment.

\paragraph{The untested cell, and a prediction.} The two axes span four combinations and
our five charts occupy only two of them: \texttt{fractional} is (split, barrier) and the
other four are all (no split, no barrier). \textbf{Nothing we ran is (split, no barrier)}
--- and that cell should, on the argument above, beat everything in the table.

It is not hypothetical. \textsc{Kine} (Levis et al.), the reference implementation for
this class of problem, uses exactly it: a dedicated $m_\ell$ field, as in fractional, but
with the direction components bounded \emph{independently} rather than normalized,
\begin{equation}
  m_\ell = \texttt{scaling\_ml}\cdot\sigma(m_{\mathrm{raw}}-\texttt{outshift}),
  \qquad
  \tilde{s},\,\tilde{c} = 2\big(\sigma(\cdot)-\tfrac12\big) \in [-1,1]
  \ \ \text{(independent, \emph{not} unit-normalized)},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  Q = -\,I\,m_\ell\,\tilde{s}, \qquad U = I\,m_\ell\,\tilde{c}.
\end{equation}
Dropping the normalization removes the barrier while keeping the split; the price is that
$P = I\,m_\ell\sqrt{\tilde{s}^2+\tilde{c}^2} \le \sqrt2\,\texttt{scaling\_ml}\,I$, so
$P\le I$ is no longer exact --- though at \textsc{Kine}'s $\texttt{scaling\_ml}=0.75$ the
bound is $\sqrt2\cdot0.75 = 1.06\,I$, i.e.\ tight to $6\%$ and arguably deliberate. That
we converged on \textsc{Kine}'s chart up to this one detail is independent corroboration;
that the detail we added is the one costing us \texttt{varbeta2} is the actionable part.
\textbf{Prediction: fractional without the unit normalization should match the
barrier-free charts on \texttt{varbeta2} while keeping fractional's \texttt{hs-pol}, and
so pass 4/4 with the best worst-case in the table.} If it does not, the split hypothesis
above is wrong and should be discarded.

\section{The two priors, and their scales}
\label{sec:dyn}

Polarization needs no prior. Stokes~$I$ needs two, and \emph{both were being used at
the wrong magnitude}.

\paragraph{Compactness was $10$--$30\times$ too weak.} The array has no zero-spacing
baseline, so large-scale flux is unconstrained and leaks into an off-source haze.
Sweeping \texttt{--compact-weight} at \texttt{--frequencies 4} improves NRMSE$_I$, the
on-ring variability, and the off-source haze \emph{monotonically and together}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\texttt{--compact-weight} & NRMSE$_I$ & dyn.\ on-ring (truth 0.355) & dyn.\ off-source (truth 0.003) \\
\midrule
0    & 0.286 & 0.152 & 0.682 \\
3    & 0.219 & 0.210 & 0.379 \\
\textbf{10}   & \textbf{0.169} & 0.267 & 0.161 \\
30   & 0.189 & \textbf{0.308} & \textbf{0.125} \\
100  & 0.542 & 0.579 (overshoot) & 0.156 \\
300  & 0.814 & 0.626 (overshoot) & 0.213 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The curve turns over past 30: at 100--300 the reconstruction invents \emph{more}
variability than the truth and $\chi^2$ climbs to 1.98/8.11. Higher spatial bandwidth
alone is \emph{harmful} (\texttt{--frequencies 3/4} without compactness gives
NRMSE$_I$ 0.39--0.43): it must be paired with the prior, because finer encoding lets
more variability leak off-source.

\paragraph{Dynamic-mode compactness, and why the weight is $10^{-3}$.} In
Eq.~\ref{eq:dmd}, variability at pixel $p$ comes from $|W_j(p)|$, so
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R}_{\rm dyn} \;=\; \frac{1}{r}\sum_{p,j} |W_j(p)|\;r^2(p)
\end{equation}
confines \emph{time-varying} structure to small radius while leaving the static ring
($W_0$) untouched. \texttt{--compact-weight} cannot do this: it penalizes total $I$, so
the ring ($r\!\approx\!28\,\mu$as) and the haze ($r\!>\!50\,\mu$as) differ by only
${\sim}4.6\times$ in $r^2$ and the ring is squeezed with it.

The magnitude matters more than the idea. Measured on a trained model,
$\mathcal{R}_{\rm dyn}\approx2341$ against a $\chi^2$ term of ${\sim}0.5$: at weight
1 the penalty is ${\sim}4700\times$ the data term and the cheapest solution is simply
$W\!\to\!0$, i.e.\ kill all dynamics. On \texttt{varbeta2}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccccc@{}}
\toprule
\texttt{--dyn-compact-weight} & $\chi^2$ & NRMSE$_I$ & EVPA & $\beta_2$ amp & $I$-variability (truth 0.000) \\
\midrule
off        & 0.47 & 0.390 & $27.4^\circ$ & 0.314 & 0.986 \; \textbf{(invented)} \\
\textbf{1e-3} & \textbf{0.40} & \textbf{0.120} & $\mathbf{2.1^\circ}$ & \textbf{0.993} & \textbf{0.000} \\
1e-2       & 0.41 & 0.120 & $23.1^\circ$ & 0.474 & 0.000 \\
1, 5, 20   & 7.5--9.6 & 0.34--0.51 & --- & --- & 0.00 \; \textbf{(annihilated)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The usable window is one decade wide. \textbf{Lesson: measure a penalty's magnitude
against the $\chi^2$ term before choosing its weight.} We mis-weighted three separate
priors by picking values by analogy.

\paragraph{Why one weight serves opposite purposes.} \texttt{varbeta2}'s Stokes~$I$ is
static, so its $I$-dynamics are \emph{fabricated} and must be removed;
\texttt{hsCW}'s hot spot is \emph{real} and must be kept. The same
\texttt{--dyn-compact-weight 1e-3} does both --- hsCW's on-ring variability is $0.271$
with the prior versus $0.267$ without --- because the penalty is radius-weighted and the
spot ($r\!\approx\!28\,\mu$as) and the fabricated haze ($r\!>\!50\,\mu$as) are
separable. This is what makes a single configuration possible.

\section{The $\chi^2$ floor is $0.4$, not $1$}
\label{sec:floor}

Generation calls \texttt{obs.add\_fractional\_noise(0.04)}, whose ehtim implementation
\emph{inflates $\sigma$ without adding matching noise}:
$\sigma\leftarrow\sqrt{\sigma^2+(0.04|V|)^2}$. The residual stays thermal while the
error bars grow, so \textbf{the truth itself scores $\chi^2\approx0.42$} --- reproduced
on every product by pushing the truth through the forward operator, which is also how
we validate that operator. Consequences: a $\chi^2$ gate centred on 1 can never pass
(ours is now $[0.4,2]$), and the default early stop at $\chi^2\le0.8$ sits
\emph{above} the truth's own score, halting training before convergence. Every run
reports \texttt{chi2\_truth\_floor} beside its own $\chi^2$.

\section{Metrics: two that lie, and their replacements}
\label{sec:metrics}

\paragraph{Time-averaged $\beta_2$ cancels a rotating swirl.}
\texttt{beta2\_coefficient} averages the cubes before projecting. That is correct for a
static EVPA and destructive for a rotating one: on \texttt{varbeta2} the truth's own
$|\beta_2|$ is $0.180$ per frame but $0.0137$ time-averaged. The old metric reported
$1.065$ for a good fit and $1.141$ for one whose swirl rotated \emph{backwards}
(phase correlation $-0.969$) --- both look like clean passes. \texttt{beta2\_series}
gives per-frame $\beta_2(t)$, and \texttt{beta2\_dynamics\_error} reports amplitude,
phase RMSE, and the \textbf{correlation of the unwrapped phase tracks}, which is the
only quantity that distinguishes tracking from counter-rotating. Dynamic-pol models
must be scored with it.

\paragraph{$\beta_2$ is meaningless on \texttt{hs-pol}.} There the polarization rides
on a compact orbiting spot while a static radial-EVPA ring dominates the annulus
integral, so the truth's global $\beta_2$ phase swings only ${\sim}1^\circ$: $\beta_2$
is scoring the ring, not the thing under test. \texttt{polarized\_dynamics\_nrmse}
removes each cube's time mean, cancelling static polarization and leaving exactly the
moving part. It also flags \emph{invented} pol variability against a static-pol truth
(\texttt{hsCW}: truth dynamic-pol power ${\approx}0$, reconstruction $0.0289$).

\section{What still does not work}

\paragraph{\texttt{mring+hs-pol}.} Every chart fails its polarization
(NRMSE$_Q$ 0.769--1.047, EVPA $9.6$--$19.0^\circ$) while Stokes~$I$ is fine in every one
(NRMSE$_I$ 0.156--0.235). By the valid measure, the best chart recovers $56\%$ of the
moving polarized power ($0.0239$ of a true $0.0426$; the others 40--54\%).
\textbf{This is a property of the model, not of the parameterization}: 20\%
polarization on a $0.3$~Jy spot (${\sim}0.06$~Jy polarized) that is \emph{moving},
against a 5\%-polarized static ring --- the faintest, most compact, most time-variable
polarized signal in the suite. Parameterization-shopping does not fix it.

\paragraph{NRMSE$_{Q,U}\le0.15$.} Met only on \texttt{varbeta2}, and only by
\texttt{iscaled} (0.114/0.121), \texttt{expm} (0.134/0.128) and \texttt{expm\_full}
(0.120/0.128) --- not by fractional (0.215). The target may be unrealistic for this
coverage: KINE reports the global $\angle\beta_2$ and explicitly not per-pixel EVPA.

\paragraph{Gains and D-terms.} Station gains are implemented behind
\texttt{--fit-gains} (off by default): complex, per-station, per-time, with R and L
solved separately (\texttt{--gain-hands 2}), applied to the \emph{model} visibilities
as $V_{pq}\!\leftarrow\!g_p V_{pq} g_q^{*}$ --- never divided into the data, which is
equivalent for pure gains but the only correct order once D-terms enter. Unit tests
confirm identity-gain neutrality, gradients reaching both amplitudes and phases, R/L
independence, and closure-phase invariance. \textbf{No fit has yet recovered a known
gain}; that validation (M3) is outstanding. For D-terms, note that fractional bounds
$m_\ell$ and $m_c$ \emph{independently}, so $P_{\rm tot}=I\sqrt{m_\ell^2+m_c^2}$ can
exceed $I$ once $V$ is modeled --- a non-PSD coherency matrix, exactly where leakage
solving is most fragile. \texttt{expm\_full} enforces $P\le I$ by construction and is
retained for that reason despite losing here.

\section*{Methodological note}
\label{sec:bug}

Two earlier versions of this note concluded that fractional polarization was
structurally incapable of representing $m\!=\!2$, and adopted first an $I$-scaled and
then a matrix-exponential chart to escape an ``off-source polarized haze.'' Both
conclusions were artifacts. \texttt{reconstruct\_polarized\_cubes} evaluated the sky
model \emph{eagerly} while training evaluates it under JIT, and the two do not agree:
eager flux $9.9536$~Jy/frame against jitted $2.6255$ (truth $2.7$), i.e.\ a
near-uniform ${\sim}3\times10^{-3}$/pixel offset --- about $7.3$~Jy of phantom diffuse
flux in every exported image. \emph{That} was the haze. The support prior, the radial
polarized compactness prior, the $I$-scaled and matrix-exp charts, and an $L_1$ prior
on polarized flux were all designed to suppress a rendering bug.

What made it durable is that the reported $\chi^2$ was correct while the images were
wrong: nothing compared the object being optimized against the object being measured.
Two guards now assert exactly that --- an in-run self-check recomputing $\chi^2$ from
the exported cube against the truth floor, and a regression test pinning
\emph{cube $=$ jitted field by total flux}. The flux integral is the essential part: a
uniform offset is invisible to a max-pixel comparison, which is why several rounds of
``the images are identical'' checks missed it.

The general lesson, which cost this effort several sessions: \textbf{every verdict
here that was reached by reasoning about why a component was written, rather than by
measuring how it performs, was wrong.} The results in Section~\ref{sec:results} come
from a single grid with no per-cell tuning, and it overturned the authors'
recommendations repeatedly.
