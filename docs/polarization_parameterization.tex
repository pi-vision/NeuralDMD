\title{Polarization parameterization for NeuralDMD:\\
the fractional/direct trade-off, and why we fit direct $(I,Q,U)$\\
with explicit support priors}
\author{Sgr\,A$^\ast$ dynamics / NeuralDMD polarization effort}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We record how NeuralDMD models linear polarization, and why the choice evolved.
We first fit the \emph{fractional} chart $(I,m_\ell,\xi)$, in which the physical
bounds $P\le I$ and ``no polarization without total intensity'' become a single
box constraint. That chart is exact and physical, but we found it carries a
\emph{hidden optimization obstruction}: the unit-normalized EVPA direction must
wind at twice the azimuthal EVPA mode, and gradient descent does not reach the
high-winding ($m\!\ge\!2$) configurations required by, e.g., a spiral EVPA. On a
synthetic thick-ring truth whose true EVPA is a spiral, every fractional fit
collapses to a near-uniform ($m\!\le\!1$) EVPA---median EVPA error $\sim\!40^\circ$
---regardless of network capacity or training budget. We therefore fit the
\emph{direct} chart $(I,Q,U)$, which has no winding obstruction, and re-supply the
two constraints the fractional chart gave for free as explicit, differentiable
priors. The key one is an \emph{intensity-gated support prior}
$\sum_x P(x)\,e^{-I(x)/\tau}$, with $I$ entering only as a stop-gradient
reference, which closes the off-source null-space ``haze'' a direct fit would
otherwise exploit. Two controlled diagnostics show the target is strongly
identified: the truth pushed through the forward operator matches the cross-hand
\emph{data} at the noise floor, and the data prefer the true spiral EVPA over the
best uniform EVPA by a factor $\sim\!20$ in $\chi^2$. The residual difficulty is
thus purely the reconstruction's null space, which the support prior is designed
to remove.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem}
Polarimetric VLBI inversion from sparse coverage is severely ill-posed: many
images reproduce the handful of measured cross-hand visibilities equally well.
Two failure modes bracket the design space. A \emph{free} $(Q,U)$ model can place
polarized flux anywhere the data permit---including off the source, where an
extended ``haze'' fits the cross hands to the noise floor while leaving the true
on-source structure unconstrained. A \emph{fractional} $(m_\ell,\xi)$ model is
immune to that (its polarization is tied to $I$), but, as we show below, cannot
represent the higher-order azimuthal EVPA structure that real ring sources carry.
The parameterization we adopt keeps the direct chart and removes its haze with a
prior, rather than paying the fractional chart's representational cost.

\section{Physical constraints on the Stokes vector}
At every pixel and time the Stokes vector $(I,Q,U,V)$ of physical radiation obeys
two hard constraints:
\begin{align}
  P \;\equiv\; \sqrt{Q^2+U^2+V^2} \;&\le\; I, \label{eq:ple}\\
  I=0 \;&\Rightarrow\; Q=U=V=0. \label{eq:support}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:ple} states that the polarized intensity cannot exceed the
total intensity ($m=P/I\in[0,1]$; a source cannot be more than $100\%$
polarized). Equation~\eqref{eq:support}, a corollary, forbids polarized flux
where there is no total flux. A free-$(Q,U)$ model can violate both; the
fractional chart enforces both by construction. The design question is at what
cost.

\section{Two coordinate charts for the same degrees of freedom}
A physical (linear) polarization state has three real degrees of freedom per
pixel, coordinatized as $(I,Q,U)$ \emph{or} as $(I,m_\ell,\xi)$, where $m_\ell$
is the linear polarization fraction and $\xi$ the EVPA:
\begin{equation}
  Q = m_\ell\,I\,\cos 2\xi,\qquad
  U = m_\ell\,I\,\sin 2\xi,
  \label{eq:transform}
\end{equation}
with inverse $m_\ell=\sqrt{Q^2+U^2}/I$ and $\xi=\tfrac12\operatorname{atan2}(U,Q)$.
This is a change of variables; no information is added or removed. What differs is
the shape of the physical region: in $(I,Q,U)$ the bound~\eqref{eq:ple} is the
nonlinear coupling $\sqrt{Q^2+U^2}\le I$, which free fields ignore; in
$(I,m_\ell,\xi)$ the \emph{same} bound is the box $m_\ell\in[0,1]$, and
support~\eqref{eq:support} is automatic because $Q,U\propto I$.

\section{Why the fractional chart fails: the EVPA winding obstruction}
\label{sec:winding}
The catch is in the angle. Polarization is a \emph{spin-2} field: the observable
object is the direction $(\cos2\xi,\sin2\xi)$, a unit vector that advances by
$4\pi$ (two full turns) when the EVPA $\xi$ advances by $2\pi$. The
physically common ring EVPAs---radial ($\xi=\phi$), azimuthal ($\xi=\phi+\tfrac\pi2$),
and spiral ($\xi=\phi+\text{const}$, our synthetic truth)---all have $\xi$
advancing once around the ring, so the direction field
$(\cos2\xi,\sin2\xi)$ has \textbf{winding number 2} around the ring, and the
resulting $(Q,U)$ carry azimuthal mode $m=2$ (a four-lobe pattern).

We parameterize the EVPA direction as a normalized pair of network outputs,
$(\cos2\xi,\sin2\xi)=(f_c,f_s)/\sqrt{f_c^2+f_s^2+\epsilon}$, precisely so that
$P=m_\ell|I|$ holds \emph{exactly} (Sec.~\ref{sec:direct} discusses the
alternative). But a unit field with nonzero winding around the ring forces the
raw field $(f_c,f_s)$ to \emph{vanish} somewhere it encloses; at those zeros the
normalized direction is singular and its gradient diverges. Gradient descent
initialized near a low-winding (nearly constant) direction must nucleate these
defects and cross the singular set to reach winding~2. Empirically it does not:
on the thick-ring + spiral-EVPA truth, fractional fits converge to a
near-uniform EVPA ($Q$ azimuthal power $\approx 96\%$ in $m\!=\!0$, versus the
truth's $69\%$ in $m\!=\!2$), with median EVPA error $\sim\!40^\circ$. This
persists across spatial bandwidth (EVPA network frequencies $1$--$4$), depth,
mode count, learning-rate schedule, and staged-vs-simultaneous training, and it
does \emph{not} improve as the cross-hand $\chi^2$ is driven down---indeed a
lower cross-hand $\chi^2$ can coincide with a \emph{worse} EVPA. The obstruction
is representational/topological, not a matter of under-training.

\section{The parameterization we adopt: direct $(I,Q,U)$ with support priors}
\label{sec:direct}
We fit $Q$ and $U$ as \emph{independent signed} NeuralDMD fields in Stokes-$I$
units,
\begin{equation}
  Q(x,t) = I_{\max}\,g_Q(x,t),\qquad U(x,t) = I_{\max}\,g_U(x,t),
\end{equation}
with $g_Q,g_U$ the raw (sign-carrying) sub-model outputs and $I$ its own field as
before. A four-lobe ($m\!=\!2$) $Q$ is now no harder to represent than the ring
itself: there is no angle to wind. The price is that
constraints~\eqref{eq:ple}--\eqref{eq:support} are no longer automatic, so we
add them back as differentiable penalties on the circular-basis $\chi^2$
objective ($RR,LL,RL,LR$; the likelihood is never formed on $Q/I$).

\paragraph{Intensity-gated support prior.} The dominant failure of a naive
direct fit is an off-source polarized haze: the reconstruction dumps
$P$ into the large-scale cross-hand null space (in our test, $1.2$--$1.3$~Jy of
off-ring $P$ against a true $0.09$~Jy), fitting $RL,LR$ without ever building the
correct on-ring structure. We suppress it with a soft realization
of~\eqref{eq:support},
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{supp}}
  \;=\; \Big\langle \textstyle\sum_x P(x,t)\,
        \exp\!\big(-\,\mathrm{sg}[\,|I(x,t)|\,]\,/\,\tau\,\overline{I}\big)\Big\rangle_t,
  \qquad P=\sqrt{Q^2+U^2(+V^2)},
  \label{eq:support-prior}
\end{equation}
which penalizes polarized flux wherever $I$ is faint, confining $P$ to the bright
ring (both off-ring \emph{and} the dark ring-center). Two details are essential:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{$I$ enters only through a stop-gradient} $\mathrm{sg}[\cdot]$
  (with $\overline I$ the peak of $|I|$, so $\tau$ is a dimensionless fraction of
  the peak brightness). Without it, $\partial\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{supp}}/\partial I<0$
  and the model games the penalty by \emph{inflating} $I$ to switch the gate off,
  which destroys the Stokes-$I$ reconstruction. With the stop-gradient the prior
  can only push $P$ \emph{down}, never push $I$ up. (KINE avoids this implicitly
  by freezing $I$ during polarization fitting; the stop-gradient makes the same
  guarantee hold in a joint fit.)
  \item It is \emph{intensity}-gated, not radius-gated. A radial second moment
  $\sum_x P\,r^2$ also penalizes off-source pol, but it permits---indeed
  encourages---polarization to pool in the faint ring \emph{center} (small $r$);
  empirically it mislocates the pol inward and fails to suppress the far haze.
  The intensity gate forbids the center too.
\end{enumerate}
An optional soft $P\le I$ term $\sum_x \mathrm{relu}\!\big(P-I\big)^2$
supplies~\eqref{eq:ple} in the same spirit. Together these recover, softly, the
confinement the fractional chart had by construction---without its winding cost.

\paragraph{Initialization and staging.} $I$ retains its disk-template
pretraining; $Q,U$ start small and grow only as the cross-hand data demand.
Either a staged schedule (fit $I$ on $RR,LL$ first, then $Q,U$ on the frozen $I$)
or a joint schedule with a polarization warm-up is compatible with the priors
above; the stop-gradient in~\eqref{eq:support-prior} is what makes the joint
(RIME-compatible) schedule safe for $I$.

\section{The target is strongly identified (diagnostics)}
Because a direct fit that still misses the EVPA invites the worry that the
\emph{data} lack the signal, we verify the opposite on the synthetic truth:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Consistency.} The saved truth $Q,U$, pushed through the exact
  per-frame forward operator, reproduce the measured cross-hand data at the noise
  floor ($\chi^2_{RR,LL,RL,LR}\approx0.4$); the $RL$ data carry real signal
  ($\overline{|V|}\approx0.11$~Jy, SNR $\approx 2$). Generation is correct.
  \item \textbf{Identifiability.} Replacing the truth's spiral EVPA with the
  best-fit \emph{uniform} EVPA (same $P$ magnitude) raises the cross-hand $\chi^2$
  from $\approx0.4$ to $\approx8$---a factor $\sim\!20$. The data do not merely
  admit the $m\!=\!2$ spiral; they \emph{demand} it ($\Delta\chi^2\approx7.9$).
\end{itemize}
The EVPA is therefore strongly constrained; the reason a naive direct fit still
returns a uniform EVPA is not degeneracy but the off-source haze escape route,
which is exactly what~\eqref{eq:support-prior} closes.

\section{Compatibility with ``never divide by $I$''}
The classic objection to fractional parameterizations---dividing by a vanishing
$I$---never applied to our fractional chart (it \emph{multiplies},
$Q=m_\ell I\cos2\xi$) and applies even less to the direct chart, where $Q,U$ are
primary fields. In both charts the likelihood is formed on the absolute Stokes /
circular visibilities $RR,LL,RL,LR$; no quantity requiring $Q/I$ ever enters.

\section{Summary}
The fractional chart $(I,m_\ell,\xi)$ makes $P\le I$ and polarization support into
box constraints, but its unit-normalized EVPA direction cannot be optimized onto
the winding-2 configurations that ordinary ring EVPAs (radial/azimuthal/spiral)
require; the fit collapses to a near-uniform EVPA. We fit the direct chart
$(I,Q,U)$, which has no such obstruction, and re-impose support and $P\le I$ as
soft priors---chiefly an intensity-gated support penalty~\eqref{eq:support-prior}
with a stop-gradient reference on $I$---to close the off-source null-space haze
that a direct fit would otherwise exploit. Diagnostics confirm the synthetic
target is consistent with the data and strongly identified, so the reconstruction
task is well posed once the haze escape is removed.
