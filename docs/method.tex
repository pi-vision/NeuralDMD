\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We extend \neuralDMD to full polarization. A single spatial network and a single
bank of temporal frequencies serve every Stokes parameter; only the readouts --- a
set of spatial modes and complex amplitudes per field --- are separate. The sharing
is the physical statement that structure and rates belong to the emitting plasma
rather than to the Stokes parameter one happens to measure. Polarization is built through a matrix-exponential chart, enforcing
$P\le I$ and support by construction rather than by penalty. Station gains are
solved jointly and the total flux is supplied from the data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model}

\paragraph{Modal representation.}
A linear dynamical system admits a decomposition into spatial modes evolving
independently in time,
\begin{equation}
  I(x,y,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{r} w_j(x,y)\, a_j(t),
  \qquad a_j(t) = b_j e^{\Omega_j t},
  \label{eq:modal}
\end{equation}
with $\Omega_j = \alpha_j + i\omega_j$ complex spectral coefficients whose real and
imaginary parts encode decay rates and oscillation frequencies. \neuralDMD
parameterizes each ingredient by a network with weights
$\Theta=[\Theta_w,\Theta_\Omega,\Theta_b]$,
\begin{equation}
  w_j(x,y) = w_j(x,y;\Theta_w), \qquad
  \Omega_j = \Omega_j(\Theta_\Omega), \qquad
  b_j = b_j(\Theta_b),
  \label{eq:params}
\end{equation}
the spatial modes being a coordinate MLP evaluable at any $(x,y)$.

\paragraph{One network for all Stokes parameters.}
The four fields we need --- intensity plus three for polarization
(Sec.~\ref{sec:chart}) --- are different projections of the same emitting plasma,
so their structure lives in the same places. A \emph{single} coordinate network
$W$ therefore produces the spatial modes of all of them at once,
\begin{equation}
  W:\ (x,y)\ \longmapsto\
  \bigl(\, w^{I}_j(x,y),\ w^{q}_j(x,y),\ w^{u}_j(x,y),\ w^{v}_j(x,y)\,\bigr)_{j=0,\dots,r}.
  \label{eq:net}
\end{equation}
This is the polarized counterpart of a
single coordinate network mapping $(x,y,t)$ to the Stokes parameters, with one
essential difference: our network does not take time as an input. The time
dependence is supplied by the modal sum below, so the spectrum $\Omega_j$ is an
explicit, readable output rather than something implicit in a black box. Sharing
one network across Stokes is the physical statement that polarized structure
appears where intensity structure appears --- a prior carried by the architecture,
not by a penalty. What stays per-field is only the modes it reads out and their
amplitudes.

\paragraph{The four fields.}
Each field is real, so its complex modes come in conjugate pairs and the field is
the real part of a modal sum. We use four such fields --- the total intensity $I$
and three fields $q,u,v$ that the chart below turns into $Q,U,V$ --- and,
crucially, the \emph{same} spectrum $\Omega_j$ drives all four:
\begin{align}
  I(x,y,t) &= (I_{\max}-I_{\min})\Big[\,w^{I}_0\,b^{I}_0
     + 2\,\Re\!\sum_{j=1}^{r} w^{I}_j\,b^{I}_j\,e^{\Omega_j \tsc t}\,\Big] + I_{\min},
     \label{eq:fieldI}\\[2pt]
  q(x,y,t) &= w^{q}_0\,b^{q}_0
     + 2\,\Re\!\sum_{j=1}^{r} w^{q}_j\,b^{q}_j\,e^{\Omega_j \tsc t},
     \label{eq:fieldq}\\[2pt]
  u(x,y,t) &= w^{u}_0\,b^{u}_0
     + 2\,\Re\!\sum_{j=1}^{r} w^{u}_j\,b^{u}_j\,e^{\Omega_j \tsc t},
     \label{eq:fieldu}\\[2pt]
  v(x,y,t) &= w^{v}_0\,b^{v}_0
     + 2\,\Re\!\sum_{j=1}^{r} w^{v}_j\,b^{v}_j\,e^{\Omega_j \tsc t}.
     \label{eq:fieldv}
\end{align}
The $j=0$ term is a real static component ($\Omega_0=0$); time is normalized to
$[0,1]$ over the observing window and $\tsc=200$ sets the range of representable
rates. The intensity is scaled to physical units in place; $q,u,v$ feed the chart
raw. That the four lines share one $\Omega_j$ is the temporal half of the model's
sharing --- the spatial half being the one network $W$ --- and only the modes $w$
and amplitudes $b$ differ between fields. Those are the physics we read off: which
spatial pattern and which harmonic each field occupies, and with what relative
phase. Sec.~\ref{sec:sharing} argues for the shared spectrum.

\paragraph{Constraints imposed by construction.}
The spectrum is bounded so that no mode grows without limit, and band-limited,
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_j = -\,\sigma(\cdot)\in[-1,0], \qquad
  \omega_j = \omega_{\min}+\sigma(\cdot)\,(\omega_{\max}-\omega_{\min}),
  \label{eq:spectrum}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma$ the logistic function ($\alpha_j\in[-200,0]$, $\omega_j\in[0,200]$
after the $\tsc$ scaling). Each field in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:fieldI}--\eqref{eq:fieldv} is invariant under
$w_j\to c\,w_j$, $b_j\to b_j/c$; this gauge is fixed by normalizing each mode to
unit RMS over the $P$ pixels,
\begin{equation}
  w_j \;\longleftarrow\; w_j \Big/ \Big(\tfrac{1}{P}\textstyle\sum_{p}|w_j(x_p,y_p)|^2\Big)^{1/2},
  \label{eq:gauge}
\end{equation}
holding the norm constant under differentiation. This is what makes $|b_j|$
comparable across modes and across Stokes parameters. The amplitudes are kept
positive and polar,
\begin{equation}
  b_0=\mathrm{softplus}(\cdot),\qquad
  b_j=\varepsilon_b\,\mathrm{softplus}(\cdot)\;e^{\,i\pi\tanh(\cdot)},
  \label{eq:amplitudes}
\end{equation}
with the amplitude network zero-initialized, so training starts from a static image
and grows dynamics only as the data demand ($\varepsilon_b=0.1$).

\section{Polarization}

\paragraph{Requirements.}
The sky is a Stokes vector obeying, at every pixel,
\begin{equation}
  P \equiv \sqrt{Q^2+U^2+V^2}\;\le\;I,
  \label{eq:pli}
\end{equation}
with polarization vanishing wherever there is no emission. Free $(Q,U,V)$ fields
respect neither, and can reach $\chi^2\approx1$ on unphysical images.

\paragraph{Chart.}
\label{sec:chart}
The polarization is built from the three fields $q,u,v$ of
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:fieldq}--\eqref{eq:fieldv} by the matrix-exponential fractional
form. With
\begin{equation}
  p=\sqrt{q^2+u^2+v^2},
  \label{eq:pmag}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;(Q,U,V) \;=\; I\,\frac{\tanh p}{p}\,\bigl(q,\,u,\,v\bigr).\;}
  \label{eq:expm}
\end{equation}
The polarization fraction is $m=\tanh p\le1$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:pli} holds
\emph{exactly} with no penalty weight; support is inherited from the factor $I$, so
polarization cannot appear off-source; $\tanh(p)/p$ is smooth through $p=0$; and
Stokes $V$ is native. The polarization angle follows as
$\chi=\tfrac12\operatorname{atan2}(U,Q)$. The obvious alternative --- a fraction
times a unit direction, $Q,U=m_\ell I(\cos2\chi,\sin2\chi)$ --- is not equivalent:
its direction field must wind $m$ times around the source for an azimuthal pattern
of order $m$, and winding number cannot change continuously during optimization,
so the fit either begins in the correct topological sector or never reaches it.

\paragraph{One spectrum, many observables.}
\label{sec:sharing}
Temporal frequencies are properties of the underlying flow, not of the Stokes
parameter one happens to measure, so a single orbiting feature imprints one
harmonic ladder on all of them. What differs is the weighting. Linear polarization
$\mathcal{P}=Q+iU$ has spin weight $2$: under a rotation by $\varphi$,
$I\to I(\theta-\varphi)$ but
$\mathcal{P}\to e^{2i\varphi}\mathcal{P}(\theta-\varphi)$, so for a pattern
rotating rigidly at $\Omega_{\mathrm{orb}}$ the azimuthal order $m$ of each field
oscillates at
\begin{equation}
  \omega^{(I)}_m = m\,\Omega_{\mathrm{orb}},
  \qquad
  \omega^{(\mathcal{P})}_m = (m-2)\,\Omega_{\mathrm{orb}} .
  \label{eq:spin2}
\end{equation}
Sharing $\Omega_j$ while keeping the amplitudes $b^{I}_j,b^{q}_j,b^{u}_j,b^{v}_j$ separate encodes what is common (the
ladder) and leaves free what is not (which rungs each Stokes parameter occupies,
and with what phase). It also pools the signal-to-noise of all four products onto
the hardest parameters to identify, which matters because the cross hands carry a
small fraction of the parallel-hand sensitivity. The index $j$ then means the same
frequency in every
field, so per-mode polarimetry is well defined: $|b^{q}_j|$ (and likewise
$u,v$) is the harmonic weight of that field, and $\arg b^{q}_j-\arg b^{I}_j$ the
phase lag between polarization and intensity. Over a window of $T$ hours the fitted
frequencies convert to physical rates as
\begin{equation}
  \omega^{\mathrm{phys}}_j = \omega_j\,\tsc/T \quad [\mathrm{rad\,hr^{-1}}],
  \label{eq:physfreq}
\end{equation}
so an orbital period appears at $2\pi/T_{\mathrm{orb}}$ with its harmonics. The
\emph{sense} of rotation is not in the spectrum ($\omega_j\ge0$, conjugate partners
implicit) but in the azimuthal phase gradient of $\arg w_j$.

\section{Forward model}

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the whole chain. An interferometer samples the
Fourier transform of the sky. Writing $A_t$ for the operator mapping frame $t$ to
its sampled visibilities, it maps each Stokes image to its visibilities,
$\hat V^{I}_t=A_t\,I_t$ and likewise $\hat V^{Q}_t,\hat V^{U}_t,\hat V^{V}_t$;
circular feeds combine them into the measured correlation products
\begin{equation}
  \hat V^{RR}=\hat V^{I}+\hat V^{V},\quad
  \hat V^{LL}=\hat V^{I}-\hat V^{V},\quad
  \hat V^{RL}=\hat V^{Q}+i\hat V^{U},\quad
  \hat V^{LR}=\hat V^{Q}-i\hat V^{U}.
  \label{eq:products}
\end{equation}
Station-based complex gains are applied to the \emph{model},
\begin{equation}
  \hat V^{\,pq}_{mn}(t)\;\longleftarrow\;
  g_{p,m}(t)\;\hat V^{\,pq}_{mn}(t)\;\overline{g_{q,n}(t)},
  \label{eq:rime}
\end{equation}
for stations $m,n$ and feed hands $p,q\in\{R,L\}$ selected by the product.
Corrupting the model rather than dividing the data is equivalent for pure gains,
but only this order stays correct once leakage enters. Amplitudes are bounded
smoothly and phases referenced to a station $m_0$,
\begin{equation}
  |g_m| = g^{\mathrm{lo}}_m+\bigl(g^{\mathrm{hi}}_m-g^{\mathrm{lo}}_m\bigr)\sigma(\cdot),
  \qquad
  \arg g_m \;\to\; \arg g_m-\arg g_{m_0},
  \label{eq:gains}
\end{equation}
with per-station bounds set by that station's known calibration quality, so a gain
at its bound can still return; a hard clip would kill the gradient there.
Referencing the phase removes the degeneracy between a global phase and a
translation of the image, which also means the absolute position of a
self-calibrated reconstruction is undetermined.

\section{Objective}

The data term is a reduced $\chi^2$ per correlation product, counted per real
degree of freedom (each complex visibility carries a real and an imaginary part;
flagged baselines are masked by $m_{tk}\in\{0,1\}$):
\begin{equation}
  \chi^2_p = \frac{\sum_{t,k} m_{tk}\,\bigl|V^p_{tk}-\hat V^p_{tk}\bigr|^2/\sigma_{tk}^2}
                  {2\sum_{t,k} m_{tk}},
  \label{eq:chi2}
\end{equation}
so a model fitting at the noise level gives $\chi^2\approx1$. The full objective is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\Theta)=\sum_{p\in\{RR,LL,RL,LR\}}\chi^2_p
  \;+\;\lambda_{\mathrm{neg}}R_{\mathrm{neg}}
  \;+\;\lambda_{\mathrm{cmp}}R_{\mathrm{cmp}},
  \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathrm{neg}}=\sum_{p,t}\bigl[\max(0,-I_{pt})\bigr]^2,
  \qquad
  R_{\mathrm{cmp}}=\Bigl\langle\textstyle\sum_p \max(0,I_{pt})\,\hat r_p^2\Bigr\rangle_t,
  \label{eq:penalties}
\end{equation}
with $\hat r_p^2=r_p^2/\langle r^2\rangle$ the normalized squared radius and
$\langle\cdot\rangle_t$ a mean over frames. Negativity applies to $I$ alone, since
$Q,U,V$ are signed. Compactness suppresses the off-source haze the short baselines
cannot constrain; it is the \emph{only} empirically chosen weight, and acts on
Stokes $I$ alone --- polarization needs none, since Eq.~\eqref{eq:expm} supplies
$P\le I$ and support. Amplitude and closure-phase $\chi^2$ are computed but
excluded from $\mathcal{L}$; being gain-invariant they are a calibration canary.

\paragraph{Flux is measured, not fitted.}
Total flux is degenerate with the overall gain amplitude and the array has no
zero-spacing baseline, so leaving it free lets a fit trade brightness against
gains. It is directly measurable: dishes at a common site (ALMA--APEX, SMA--JCMT)
form baselines of metres, on which any source is unresolved, so
\begin{equation}
  F_t = \bigl|V_{mn}(t)\bigr| \quad\text{for co-located } m,n.
  \label{eq:lightcurve}
\end{equation}
A real source varies, so $F_t$ is a curve. It is imposed structurally by rescaling
every Stokes parameter at each frame,
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(I,Q,U,V\bigr)_{pt}\;\longleftarrow\;
  \frac{F_t}{\sum_{p'}I_{p't}}\,\bigl(I,Q,U,V\bigr)_{pt},
  \label{eq:fixflux}
\end{equation}
removing the degree of freedom entirely; being a common factor, it leaves every
polarization fraction untouched.

\section{Validation}

The numbers below were obtained with a per-field spatial network rather than the
single shared network of Eq.~\eqref{eq:net}; a per-Stokes network was used
instead, with the shared spectrum in use throughout. Evaluation of the
single-network variant is in progress.

On synthetic polarized truths observed with EHT coverage the model recovers the
polarization structure with NRMSE$_Q\approx0.11$ and a polarization-angle error
near $1^\circ$, and the recovered frequencies fall on harmonics of the injected
orbit through Eq.~\eqref{eq:physfreq}. Giving each field private modes tests the
assumption behind Eqs.~\eqref{eq:fieldI}--\eqref{eq:fieldv} rather than asserting it: with $6$ of $8$
modes shared, the polarization fields place $100\%$ of their power on the shared
modes and Stokes $I$ retains $30\%$ on its private ones.

On a real EHT validation-ladder observation (GRMHD with an injected hot spot, 7
stations, 99 snapshots over $3.15$\,hr) the data are not phase calibrated: the
ground truth itself scores $\chi^2_{\mathrm{vis}}=1.2\times10^5$ while scoring
$\chi^2_{\mathrm{cphase}}=0.999$. Solving Eq.~\eqref{eq:rime} jointly and fixing
the flux through Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixflux}, the reconstruction reproduces the
gain-invariant observables at the floor the data permit, in both total intensity
and polarization:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
 & $\chi^2_{\mathrm{cphase}}$ & $\chi^2_{\mathrm{logcamp}}$ & $\chi^2_{\breve m}$ \\
\midrule
ground truth (floor) & 0.999 & 0.884 & 0.923 \\
this work            & 1.31  & 1.28  & 0.82 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The image domain is a weaker statement. Eq.~\eqref{eq:gains} leaves absolute
position undetermined, and the reconstruction sits $28\,\mu$as from the truth;
after aligning on Stokes $I$, NRMSE$_I=0.62$ with a cross-correlation of $0.86$,
but NRMSE$_Q=0.81$, NRMSE$_U=0.74$ and the polarization-angle error is $14^\circ$
--- far from the ${\sim}1^\circ$ reached on the synthetic truths. The polarized
\emph{visibilities} are therefore fitted to the noise level while the polarized
\emph{image} is not pinned down: with seven stations the cross-hand null space is
large, and a GRMHD source is not as compactly described by $r$ modes as the
parametric truths are. Reducing that gap is the open problem.
