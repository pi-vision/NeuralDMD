\large Dynamic modes of a polarized black-hole movie}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
\neuralDMD represents a movie as a small set of dynamic modes: a static image plus
$r$ complex spatial modes, each oscillating and decaying at its own complex
frequency. This note extends it to full polarization $(I,Q,U,V)$ and writes out
the complete model. The central design question is how much temporal structure
the Stokes parameters should share, and we answer it by measurement: the
polarization fields are given a \emph{shared} bank of frequencies alongside
\emph{private} modes of their own, and the fitted amplitudes then report how much
of the polarized variability actually lives on the shared bank. We also give the
calibration forward model and the full objective, separating the terms that
encode physics from the ones that are tuned by hand.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background: \neuralDMD for Stokes $I$}

\subsection{Modal representation}

A linear dynamical system admits a modal decomposition, in which a
spatio-temporal field is a low-rank superposition of spatial modes evolving
independently in time,
\begin{equation}
  I(x,y,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{r} w_j(x,y)\, a_j(t),
  \qquad a_j(t) = b_j e^{\Omega_j t},
  \label{eq:modal}
\end{equation}
where $w_j(x,y)$ are the spatial modes, $b_j$ are coefficients set by the initial
state, and $\Omega_j = \alpha_j + i\omega_j$ are complex spectral coefficients
whose real and imaginary parts encode decay rates and oscillation frequencies.

\neuralDMD parameterizes every ingredient of Eq.~\eqref{eq:modal} by a network,
with trainable weights $\Theta=[\Theta_w,\Theta_\Omega,\Theta_b]$:
\begin{equation}
  w_j(x,y) = w_j(x,y;\Theta_w), \qquad
  \Omega_j = \Omega_j(\Theta_\Omega), \qquad
  b_j = b_j(\Theta_b).
  \label{eq:params}
\end{equation}
The spatial modes are a coordinate MLP, so they can be evaluated at arbitrary
$(x,y)$; the spectrum and the amplitudes come from two small networks acting on
learned latent vectors.

\subsection{Real-valued reconstruction}

The field is real, so the complex modes occur in conjugate pairs and the
reconstruction is formed from the real part of the modal sum,
\begin{equation}
  \hat I(x,y,t)= w_0(x,y)\,b_0
  \;+\; 2\,\Re\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r} w_j(x,y)\,b_j\,e^{\Omega_j \tsc t}\right),
  \label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
where the $j=0$ term is a real static component ($\Omega_0=0$) capturing the
time-averaged signal. Time $t$ is normalized to $[0,1]$ over the observing
window, and the constant $\tsc$ (we use $\tsc=200$) places the representable
rates in a useful range.

\subsection{Constraints carried by the architecture}

Three constraints are imposed by construction rather than by penalty.

\paragraph{Bounded spectrum.} The real part is held non-positive so that no mode
grows without bound, and the frequency is band-limited:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_j = -\,\sigma(\cdot) \in [-1,0], \qquad
  \omega_j = \omega_{\min} + \sigma(\cdot)\,(\omega_{\max}-\omega_{\min}),
  \label{eq:spectrum_bounds}
\end{equation}
with $\sigma$ the logistic function applied to unconstrained network outputs.
After the $\tsc$ scaling these correspond to $\alpha_j\in[-200,0]$ and
$\omega_j\in[0,200]$ over the window.

\paragraph{Mode gauge.} Equation~\eqref{eq:recon} is invariant under
$w_j \to c\,w_j$, $b_j \to b_j/c$. This freedom is removed by normalizing each
mode to unit RMS over the $P$ image pixels,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde w_j(x,y) = \frac{w_j(x,y)}{\big\langle |w_j|^2 \big\rangle^{1/2}},
  \qquad
  \big\langle |w_j|^2 \big\rangle = \frac{1}{P}\sum_{p=1}^{P} |w_j(x_p,y_p)|^2 ,
  \label{eq:gauge}
\end{equation}
with the norm treated as a constant when differentiating. This is what makes the
amplitudes $|b_j|$ comparable across modes and across Stokes parameters, and it is
what makes any statement about the mode spectrum well posed.

\paragraph{Amplitudes.} The static amplitude is kept positive and the dynamic
amplitudes are written in polar form,
\begin{equation}
  b_0 = \mathrm{softplus}(\cdot), \qquad
  b_j = \rho_j\, e^{i\phi_j},\quad
  \rho_j = \varepsilon_b\,\mathrm{softplus}(\cdot),\quad
  \phi_j = \pi \tanh(\cdot),
  \label{eq:amplitudes}
\end{equation}
with the amplitude head zero-initialized, so training starts from a static image
and grows dynamics only as the data demand them ($\varepsilon_b = 0.1$).

\subsection{Measurement}

An interferometer samples the Fourier transform of the sky. The visibility at
baseline $(u,v)$ and time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  V(u,v,t) = \mathcal{F}[I_t](u,v),
  \label{eq:visibility}
\end{equation}
measured only on a sparse, time-varying set $\mathcal{S}_t=\{(u_k,v_k,t_k)\}$.
Writing $A_t$ for the discrete operator that maps the image vector at frame $t$
to its sampled visibilities, the model prediction is
\begin{equation}
  \hat V_t = A_t\, \hat I_t ,
  \label{eq:forward}
\end{equation}
and the parameters are fitted by
\begin{equation}
  \Theta^{\star} = \argmin_{\Theta}\ \chi^2(\Theta),
  \qquad
  \chi^{2}(\Theta) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
  \frac{\bigl|V_i-\hat V_i(\Theta)\bigr|^{2}}{\sigma_i^{2}} .
  \label{eq:chi2_paper}
\end{equation}
In this work we count degrees of freedom explicitly: each complex visibility
carries a real and an imaginary part, and flagged baselines are masked, so
\begin{equation}
  \chi^2 = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{t,k} m_{tk}\,
                 \bigl|V_{tk}-\hat V_{tk}\bigr|^2 \big/ \sigma_{tk}^2}
                {\displaystyle 2\sum_{t,k} m_{tk}} ,
  \qquad m_{tk}\in\{0,1\},
  \label{eq:chi2}
\end{equation}
so that a model fitting at the noise level gives $\chi^2\approx1$.

\section{Polarization}

\subsection{What has to be represented}

The sky is a Stokes vector $(I,Q,U,V)$ obeying, at every pixel,
\begin{equation}
  P \equiv \sqrt{Q^2+U^2+V^2} \;\le\; I ,
  \label{eq:pli}
\end{equation}
with polarization vanishing wherever there is no emission. A telescope with
circular feeds measures the four correlation products
\begin{equation}
  RR = I+V, \qquad LL = I-V, \qquad RL = Q+iU, \qquad LR = Q-iU ,
  \label{eq:circular}
\end{equation}
so that $I=\tfrac12(RR+LL)$ and $V=\tfrac12(RR-LL)$, while the linear
polarization appears only in the cross hands. The linear polarization is
naturally one \emph{complex} field,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{P} \equiv Q+iU = m_\ell\,I\,e^{2i\chi},
  \qquad \chi = \tfrac12\arg\mathcal{P} = \tfrac12\operatorname{atan2}(U,Q),
  \label{eq:pdef}
\end{equation}
where $m_\ell\in[0,1]$ is the linear polarization fraction and $\chi$ the
polarization angle (EVPA).

\subsection{Spin weight, and what it implies for the spectrum}

$\mathcal{P}$ has spin weight $2$: rotating the source by an angle $\varphi$
takes $I(r,\theta)\to I(r,\theta-\varphi)$ but
$\mathcal{P}\to e^{2i\varphi}\mathcal{P}(r,\theta-\varphi)$. Decomposing both in
azimuthal harmonics and letting the pattern rotate rigidly at
$\Omega_{\mathrm{orb}}$, the order-$m$ component of each field oscillates at
\begin{equation}
  \omega^{(I)}_m = m\,\Omega_{\mathrm{orb}},
  \qquad
  \omega^{(\mathcal{P})}_m = (m-2)\,\Omega_{\mathrm{orb}} .
  \label{eq:spin2}
\end{equation}
Intensity and polarization therefore draw their frequencies from the \emph{same}
harmonic ladder but weight its rungs differently --- the intensity of an orbiting
feature peaks at the fundamental, the polarization at the second harmonic. A
second consequence is that $\mathcal{P}$ has a definite sense of rotation, so its
temporal spectrum is one-sided, unlike that of the real field $I$, whose spectrum
is conjugate-symmetric by construction.

\subsection{Per-Stokes fields}

Each raw field is an independent \neuralDMD field of the form of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:recon}. Writing $\SP$ for a raw field label,
\begin{equation}
  f^{\SP}(x,y,t) = w^{\SP}_0(x,y)\,b^{\SP}_0
  \;+\; 2\,\Re\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r} w^{\SP}_j(x,y)\,b^{\SP}_j\,
        e^{\Omega^{\SP}_j \tsc t}\right).
  \label{eq:field}
\end{equation}
The Stokes parameters are then built pointwise from these fields by a chart
(Sec.~\ref{sec:charts}). The intensity field is scaled to physical units,
\begin{equation}
  I(x,y,t) = f^{I}(x,y,t)\,(I_{\max}-I_{\min}) + I_{\min},
  \label{eq:iscale}
\end{equation}
while the fields entering the polarization charts are used raw and bounded by
their own activations.

\subsection{Shared and private temporal modes}
\label{sec:partition}

Physics argues that the frequencies belong to the flow rather than to the
observable, so one orbiting feature should imprint one ladder on every Stokes
parameter. Statistics agrees: the cross hands carry a small fraction of the
parallel-hand signal-to-noise, and frequencies are the hardest parameters to
identify from them. But Eq.~\eqref{eq:spin2} says the \emph{weights} differ, and
nothing guarantees that a real source has no polarization-only variability.

Rather than assume an answer, we make the spectrum a partition. One designated
field owns a bank $\Omega^{\mathrm{sh}}$, and each coupled field uses
\begin{equation}
  \Omega^{\SP}_j =
  \begin{cases}
    \Omega^{\mathrm{sh}}_j, & j < n_{\mathrm{sh}},\\[2pt]
    \Omega^{\SP,\mathrm{own}}_j, & j \ge n_{\mathrm{sh}},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:partition}
\end{equation}
so $n_{\mathrm{sh}}=0$ recovers fully independent fields, $n_{\mathrm{sh}}=r$
shares the whole bank, and anything between leaves each field private modes. The
amplitudes $b^{\SP}_j$ are \emph{never} shared: the per-Stokes weighting of the
harmonics, and the relative phases between Stokes parameters, are exactly the
physical content we want to measure. Two scopes are useful --- sharing among the
polarization fields only (they are components of one spin-2 field), or sharing
with Stokes $I$ as well (which is what makes the mode index mean the same
frequency in every Stokes parameter).

Because private modes remain available, the fit reports whether they were needed.
Define the shared power fraction of a field as
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R}^{\SP}
  = \frac{\sum_{j<n_{\mathrm{sh}}} |b^{\SP}_j|^2}
         {\sum_{j} |b^{\SP}_j|^2} \in [0,1].
  \label{eq:sharedpower}
\end{equation}
A value near unity says the polarized variability lives on the intensity's
ladder; a small value says it does not, which is a statement about the source.

\subsection{Recovering physical rates}

The fitted frequencies are dimensionless. With the normalized time axis spanning
a window of $T$ hours,
\begin{equation}
  \omega_j^{\mathrm{phys}} = \frac{\omega_j\,\tsc}{T}
  \quad [\mathrm{rad\,hr^{-1}}],
  \label{eq:physfreq}
\end{equation}
so an orbital period $T_{\mathrm{orb}}$ appears at $2\pi/T_{\mathrm{orb}}$
together with its harmonics. Since $\omega_j\ge0$ and the conjugate partners are
implicit in Eq.~\eqref{eq:recon}, the \emph{sense} of rotation is not encoded in
the spectrum: it lives in the azimuthal phase gradient of $\arg w_j$, and any
claim about the direction of motion must be read from the spatial modes.

\section{Polarization charts}
\label{sec:charts}

Free $(Q,U,V)$ fields satisfy neither Eq.~\eqref{eq:pli} nor the support
requirement. We therefore build the polarized sky through one of five charts. All
consume the same raw fields $f^{\SP}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:field}; only the pointwise
map to Stokes differs. Below, $\sigma$ is the logistic function and $\delta$ an
output bias that starts the source unpolarized.

\paragraph{(a) \texttt{fractional}.} A polarization fraction times a unit
direction:
\begin{equation}
  m_\ell = m^{\max}_\ell\,\sigma\!\left(f^{m}-\delta\right),
  \qquad
  (\cos2\chi,\ \sin2\chi) = \frac{(f^{c},\,f^{s})}{\sqrt{(f^{c})^2+(f^{s})^2}},
  \label{eq:frac_dir}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  Q = m_\ell\,I\cos2\chi, \qquad
  U = m_\ell\,I\sin2\chi, \qquad
  V = m_c\,I,\quad m_c = 2\sigma\!\left(f^{v}-\delta\right)-1 .
  \label{eq:fractional}
\end{equation}
Because the direction is unit-normalized, $P=m_\ell|I|$ \emph{exactly}, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pli} holds strictly for $m_\ell^{\max}\le1$.

\paragraph{(b) \texttt{direct}.} Independent signed fields in intensity units,
\begin{equation}
  Q = I_{\max} f^{q}, \qquad U = I_{\max} f^{u}, \qquad V = I_{\max} f^{v},
  \label{eq:direct}
\end{equation}
with no tie to $I$; Eq.~\eqref{eq:pli} and support must then be supplied by
penalties.

\paragraph{(c) \texttt{iscaled}.} Free but $I$-scaled,
\begin{equation}
  Q = I\tanh f^{q}, \qquad U = I\tanh f^{u}, \qquad V = I\tanh f^{v},
  \label{eq:iscaled}
\end{equation}
which gives support by construction and $P\le\sqrt{3}\,I$ (for linear
polarization alone, $P\le\sqrt2\,I$); the residual bound is left to a penalty.

\paragraph{(d) \texttt{expm}.} The matrix-exponential fractional form, applied on
top of our intensity field. With
\begin{equation}
  p = \sqrt{(f^{q})^2+(f^{u})^2+(f^{v})^2},
  \label{eq:pmag}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  (Q,U,V) = I\,\frac{\tanh p}{p}\,\bigl(f^{q},f^{u},f^{v}\bigr).
  \label{eq:expm}
\end{equation}
The fraction is $m=\tanh p\le1$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:pli} holds \emph{exactly} with
no penalty weight; support is inherited from the factor $I$; and
$\tanh(p)/p$ is smooth through $p=0$, so there is no obstruction at zero
polarization. This chart is native in Stokes $V$.

\paragraph{(e) \texttt{expm\_full}.} The full coherency matrix as a matrix
exponential,
\begin{equation}
  X = \exp
  \begin{pmatrix}
    s+f^{v} & f^{q}+if^{u} \\
    f^{q}-if^{u} & s-f^{v}
  \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  X = \begin{pmatrix} I+V & Q+iU \\ Q-iU & I-V \end{pmatrix},
  \label{eq:expmfull_def}
\end{equation}
whose argument is Hermitian, so $X$ is positive semi-definite. Explicitly,
\begin{equation}
  I = e^{s}\cosh p, \qquad
  (Q,U,V) = e^{s}\,\frac{\sinh p}{p}\,\bigl(f^{q},f^{u},f^{v}\bigr),
  \label{eq:expmfull}
\end{equation}
so $I>0$ and $\det X = I^2-P^2 \ge 0$ hold \emph{everywhere} by construction. Here
$s$ is a log-intensity, which makes this chart incompatible with a pretrained
intensity template.

\paragraph{The winding obstruction.} Chart (a) is the only one whose direction
field is unit-normalized. An azimuthal pattern of order $m$ requires
$(\cos2\chi,\sin2\chi)$ to wind $m$ times around the origin as one circles the
source, and winding number cannot change continuously during optimization. The
fit therefore either begins in the correct topological sector or cannot reach it.
Charts (b)--(e) use free $f^{q},f^{u}$ and carry no such barrier; this turns out
to matter more than any other modelling choice we tested (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}).

\section{Instrument model}

Model visibilities are formed per Stokes parameter by Eq.~\eqref{eq:forward} and
combined into correlation products through Eq.~\eqref{eq:circular},
\begin{equation}
  \hat V^{RR} = \hat V^{I}+\hat V^{V},\quad
  \hat V^{LL} = \hat V^{I}-\hat V^{V},\quad
  \hat V^{RL} = \hat V^{Q}+i\hat V^{U},\quad
  \hat V^{LR} = \hat V^{Q}-i\hat V^{U}.
  \label{eq:products}
\end{equation}
Station-based complex gains are then applied to the \emph{model},
\begin{equation}
  \hat V^{\,pq}_{mn}(t) \;\longleftarrow\;
  g_{p,m}(t)\; \hat V^{\,pq}_{mn}(t)\; \overline{g_{q,n}(t)} ,
  \label{eq:rime}
\end{equation}
for stations $m,n$ and feed hands $p,q\in\{R,L\}$ selected by the product
($RR\!\to\!(R,R)$, $RL\!\to\!(R,L)$, and so on). Corrupting the model rather than
dividing the data is equivalent for pure gains, but only this order remains
correct once leakage enters the chain. The amplitudes are bounded smoothly and
the phases referenced to a fixed station $m_0$,
\begin{equation}
  |g_m| = g^{\mathrm{lo}}_m + \bigl(g^{\mathrm{hi}}_m-g^{\mathrm{lo}}_m\bigr)\,
          \sigma(\cdot),
  \qquad
  \arg g_m \;\to\; \arg g_m - \arg g_{m_0},
  \label{eq:gains}
\end{equation}
with per-station bounds taken from that station's known calibration quality, so a
gain that reaches its bound can still return; a hard clip would kill the gradient
there. Referencing the phase removes the degeneracy between a global phase and a
translation of the image --- which also means the absolute position of a
self-calibrated reconstruction is not determined.

\section{Objective}

The quantity minimized is a sum of per-product $\chi^2$ terms plus image-domain
penalties,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\Theta)
  = \sum_{p\in\{RR,LL,RL,LR\}} \chi^2_p(\Theta)
  \;+\; \sum_{k} \lambda_k R_k(\Theta).
  \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
Amplitude and closure-phase $\chi^2$ are computed but never enter
$\mathcal{L}$; they are diagnostics, and the closure quantities double as a
calibration canary because they are invariant to Eq.~\eqref{eq:rime}. The
penalties, written over pixels $p$ and frames $t$, are
\begin{align}
  R_{\mathrm{neg}} &= \sum_{p,t} \bigl[\max(0,-I_{pt})\bigr]^2,
  \label{eq:pen_neg}\\[2pt]
  R_{P\le I} &= \sum_{p,t} \bigl[\max\bigl(0,\ P_{pt}-I_{pt}\bigr)\bigr]^2,
  \label{eq:pen_pli}\\[2pt]
  R_{\mathrm{flux}} &= \Bigl\langle
     \Bigl(\textstyle\sum_p I_{pt} - F_t\Bigr)^2 \big/ F_t^2
     \Bigr\rangle_t ,
  \label{eq:pen_flux}\\[2pt]
  R_{\mathrm{cmp}} &= \Bigl\langle \textstyle\sum_p \max(0,I_{pt})\;\hat r_p^2
     \Bigr\rangle_t ,
     \qquad \hat r_p^2 = r_p^2 \big/ \langle r^2\rangle ,
  \label{eq:pen_cmp}\\[2pt]
  R_{\mathrm{dyn}} &= \frac{1}{r}\sum_{p,j} \bigl|w^{I}_{j}(x_p,y_p)\bigr|\,\hat r_p^2 ,
  \label{eq:pen_dyn}\\[2pt]
  R_{\mathrm{sup}} &= \Bigl\langle \textstyle\sum_p P_{pt}\,
     e^{-|I_{pt}|/(\tau I_{\max})} \Bigr\rangle_t ,
  \label{eq:pen_sup}\\[2pt]
  R_{L_1} &= \Bigl\langle \textstyle\sum_p P_{pt} \Bigr\rangle_t ,
  \qquad
  R_{\mathrm{sp}} = \sum_{\SP}\Bigl(
     \bigl\langle|w^{\SP}_0|\bigr\rangle + \bigl\langle|w^{\SP}|\bigr\rangle
     + \bigl\langle|b^{\SP}_0|\bigr\rangle + \bigl\langle|b^{\SP}|\bigr\rangle \Bigr).
  \label{eq:pen_l1}
\end{align}
Here $r_p$ is the pixel radius from the field centre, $F_t$ is the total flux at
frame $t$, and $\langle\cdot\rangle$ denotes a mean. Negativity is applied to $I$
alone, since $Q,U,V$ are signed. In $R_{\mathrm{sup}}$ the intensity enters the
gate only as a passive reference (no gradient), so the term can push $P$ down but
never push $I$ up.

\paragraph{Flux is measured, not fitted.} Total flux is degenerate with the
overall gain amplitude, and the array has no zero-spacing baseline. It is,
however, directly measurable: dishes at a common site (ALMA--APEX, SMA--JCMT)
form baselines of metres, on which any plausible source is unresolved, so
\begin{equation}
  F_t = \bigl|V_{mn}(t)\bigr| \quad\text{for co-located } m,n .
  \label{eq:lightcurve}
\end{equation}
A real source varies, so $F_t$ is a curve, not a number. It may be used softly
through $R_{\mathrm{flux}}$, or imposed structurally by rescaling every Stokes
parameter at each frame,
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(I,Q,U,V\bigr)_{pt} \;\longleftarrow\;
  \frac{F_t}{\sum_{p'} I_{p't}} \,\bigl(I,Q,U,V\bigr)_{pt},
  \label{eq:fixflux}
\end{equation}
which removes the degree of freedom entirely and leaves every polarization
fraction untouched.

Table~\ref{tab:priors} sorts the ingredients of Eq.~\eqref{eq:total} by the kind
of justification each has: \emph{structural} (enforced by the parameterization,
no free weight), \emph{measured} (a number estimated from the data), and
\emph{hand-tuned} (a weight chosen because it worked). The last category is the
one to minimize.

\section{Architecture}

Figure~\ref{fig:arch} assembles the pieces. Three networks produce the spatial
modes, the spectrum and the amplitudes; the modal sum of Eq.~\eqref{eq:field}
combines them, a chart maps the result to Stokes parameters, and the instrument
model turns those into the measured correlation products. Only $\Theta_\Omega$ is
shared between Stokes parameters: $w^{\SP}_j$ and $b^{\SP}_j$ carry a per-Stokes
label and stay separate, which is what keeps the per-Stokes harmonic weights and
the relative phases available to be read off after the fit.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Every comparison below reuses a frozen observation, so all arms see identical
data and only the model changes. Two synthetic truths are used: \textsf{HSP}, a
weakly polarized ring with a polarized orbiting hot spot, where intensity and
polarization are both dynamic; and \textsf{VB2}, a rotating polarization pattern
on a \emph{static} ring, where polarization carries all the time dependence.
Their measured spectra sit on the common ladder of Eq.~\eqref{eq:spin2} built on
the 80-minute orbit ($\Omega_{\mathrm{orb}}=4.71$ rad\,hr$^{-1}$), with intensity
peaking at the fundamental and polarization at the second harmonic. Because two
runs of the same configuration can land in different optima, every entry is a
median over seeds with the full range in brackets.

Table~\ref{tab:charts_result} shows that the largest effect is not the coupling,
the rank, or any penalty weight, but the chart. On VB2 the \texttt{fractional}
EVPA error ranges from $1.2^\circ$ to $44.8^\circ$ across seeds of the same
configuration, while \texttt{iscaled} varies only between $0.94^\circ$ and
$0.99^\circ$. This is the winding obstruction of Sec.~\ref{sec:charts}: the
optimizer either starts in the right topological sector or cannot reach it.

The remedy costs nothing. Chart (d) obtains $P\le I$ exactly from $m=\tanh p\le1$
and support from its proportionality to $I$, both by construction and with no
penalty weight. It is a change of coordinates, not a new prior.

\subsection{Shared and private modes}

Fitting HSP with $6$ of $8$ modes shared, Eq.~\eqref{eq:sharedpower} gives
$\mathcal{R}=1.000$ for the polarization field and $\mathcal{R}=0.704$ for Stokes
$I$. On these truths the polarized variability lives entirely on the shared
ladder: polarization needs no frequency that $I$ does not already have, while $I$
retains variability that polarization does not share. (With $n_{\mathrm{sh}}=r$
the fraction is trivially unity and carries no information.)

Coupling helps where the physics says it should. On HSP, where both are dynamic,
sharing the whole bank gives the best fit ($\chi^2$ over floor $0.016$ versus
$0.077$ uncoupled at equal rank). On VB2, whose intensity is static and has no
dynamics to lend, coupling is neutral. Rank was swept alongside coupling, since a
shared bank holds $r$ frequencies where independent fields hold up to $4r$.

The split in Table~\ref{tab:knobs} is clean. \textbf{Polarization needs no image
prior at all}: NRMSE$_Q$ and EVPA are unchanged, or marginally better, with every
penalty at zero, because the chart already supplies support and $P\le I$.

factor two to three (NRMSE$_I$ from $0.139$ to $0.432$ on HSP). That is the
short-baseline null space made concrete: nothing else in Eq.~\eqref{eq:total}
penalizes an off-source haze.

The hand-tuned part of the objective therefore reduces to a \emph{single} weight,
acting on Stokes $I$ alone. Terms
$R_{P\le I}$, $R_{\mathrm{dyn}}$, $R_{\mathrm{sup}}$ and $R_{L_1}$ all go to zero
without loss, and the flux anchor moves from a tuned scalar to a measured curve.

\subsection{Real data: the EHT validation ladder}

We fit the ladder's \texttt{grmhd2+hs1} observation --- a GRMHD simulation with an
injected hot spot, on the real array (7 stations, 99 snapshots, 1976 visibilities
over 3.15\,hr).

Two properties of this data constrain what the objective may be. First, it is not
phase calibrated: scoring the ground truth against the data it generated gives
$\chi^2_{\mathrm{cphase}}=0.999$ and $\chi^2_{\mathrm{logcamp}}=0.884$ but
$\chi^2_{\mathrm{vis}}=1.2\times10^5$, so fitting complex visibilities without
solving Eq.~\eqref{eq:rime} measures the wrong thing. Second, the source varies,
its total flux swinging from 2.08 to 3.22\,Jy, so a scalar anchor would fight the
variability it is meant to constrain.

With gains and phases solved jointly and the flux fixed per frame through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixflux}:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & $\chi^2_{\mathrm{cphase}}$ & $\chi^2_{\mathrm{logcamp}}$ \\
\midrule
ground truth (floor)                 & 0.999 & 0.884 \\
this work                            & \textbf{1.306} & \textbf{1.281} \\
soft flux anchor, gains solved       & 174.8 & 17.0 \\
amplitude-only gains                 & $\sim\!10^4$ & --- \\
no calibration solved                & 184.7 & 16.6 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The gain-invariant observables are reproduced essentially at the floor the data
permits. Two steps mattered. Solving \emph{phases} is not an optimization detail:
with amplitude-only gains the fit never leaves $\chi^2\sim10^4$. And the flux
constraint had to be structural --- with a soft anchor the data term outvoted it
and the fit collapsed to negative total flux, since several hundred free gain
phases can absorb almost any sky.

The residual discrepancy is a global position offset: phase referencing in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gains} leaves absolute position undetermined, and the
reconstruction sits $28\,\mu$as from the truth. Aligning it reduces NRMSE$_I$
from $1.05$ to $0.62$ and gives a normalized cross-correlation of $0.857$. This
is expected rather than pathological, and is why alignment-tolerant metrics are
the right figure of merit for self-calibrated reconstructions.

\subsection{What is not established}

The rank $r$ was swept but not calibrated from physics. The GRMHD case is fitted,
not yet validated across the ladder, and no comparison against \kine or \resolve
on identical data has been run --- the claim is that the objective can be
simplified without loss, not that the method is better than the alternatives. The
seed spread comes from 2--8 repeats, enough to expose the bimodality of chart (a)
but not to place tight error bars between the well-behaved charts.

\section{Relation to other methods}

\kine also represents a movie with a coordinate network and also solves station
gains, but it fixes Stokes $I$ first and fits polarization on the frozen result.
That ordering forecloses a joint solution for leakage, which requires intensity
and polarization to move together. Its released implementation also carries
penalties not described in its paper: on the outer field of view, on the light
curve, on the static and dynamic flux separately, on persistent flux in the
dynamic component, and a support term functionally identical to
$R_{\mathrm{sup}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pen_sup}, which we arrived at independently.

\resolve takes the opposite stance, treating reconstruction as Bayesian inference
under explicit priors, and does solve calibration and leakage jointly. Its
strength is precisely the weight of those priors. Our contribution is therefore
neither joint calibration nor prior engineering, but the explicit mode
representation: a spectrum $\{\Omega_j\}$ with per-Stokes amplitudes
$\{b^{\SP}_j\}$ that can be compared directly against the rates of change in the
source, which neither method produces.
